Analysis

1. RESTORATION OF THE LITERARY MEANING (PHYSICAL AND BIOLOGICAL MECHANICS)

"Except for their coupled partners, or what their mutual contracts and commitments legitimately hold, for in that case, no fault is attributed to them."

The expression "mā malakat aymānuhum" (مَا مَلَكَتْ أَيْمَٰنُهُمْ) describes a strictly contractual and material mechanic.

  • Aymān (أَيْمَٰن - root Y-M-N) refers to oaths, formal commitments, or validated bilateral contracts.
  • Malakat (مَلَكَتْ - root M-L-K) refers to holding access or management rights over a resource.

In physical and social reality, this expression does not refer to a class of subhuman individuals, but to the scope of exclusive access rights generated by a private contract. The verse establishes that physical intimacy is legitimate only within two structured frameworks:

  1. Azwājihim (أَزْوَٰجِهِمْ): Public and sealed coupling (formal marriage).
  2. Mā malakat aymānuhum: Private contractual agreements, based on mutual commitment, consent, and transfer of material value, distinct from the public architecture of marriage.

2. AUDIT OF DEVIATIONS AND BREAKDOWN OF CONSENSUS

Traditional exegesis (Tafsir) and classical jurisprudence (Fiqh) have operated a major semantic distortion ('Iwaj) on this segment, generating one of the heaviest corruptions of the system:

  1. The fabrication of institutional slavery: The consensus translated "Aymān" (Y-M-N) by its literal anatomical meaning "right hands", and "Malakat" by "physical possession". The expression was thus translated as "what your right hands possess", serving as an absolute legal basis to justify slavery, the possession of human beings, and non-consensual sexual access to war captives (forced concubinage).
  2. Refutation by the root (Y-M-N): In the entirety of the Quranic corpus, when it comes to human relations and rights, the root Y-M-N (Aymān) systematically refers to oaths and contracts (e.g.: Aymānakum = your oaths). The text does not say "the humans your hands possess", but "the access rights your contracts hold". One does not possess a human being, one possesses the terms of a contract.
  3. The erasure of consent: By transforming a bilateral contract (Y-M-N) into a slave status, Fiqh annulled the necessity of mutual consent (Tarady) and material compensation (Oujour) required by the source text for any intimate interaction. The Quranic system formally prohibits coercion (Ikrah) and requires mutual protection (Ihsan), which completely invalidates the concept of a "war captive" subjected by force.

3. LOGICAL AND TOPOLOGICAL CONCLUSION

Surah 23 (Al-Mu'minun) opens with the definition of secured individuals (those who have validated the protection protocol). One of their fundamental characteristics (verse 5) is the strict preservation of their sexual and reproductive energy (the guarding of their "Furuj").

Verse 6 defines the only two authorized interfaces for releasing this energy without generating entropy (without committing a fault / ghayru malūmīn):

  • The public interface: Symmetrical coupling (Azwāj).
  • The private interface: Mutual contractual agreement (Mā malakat aymānuhum).

Any dissipation of energy outside these two structured containers is classified in verse 7 as a transgression (B-GH-Y / exceeding limits). The system does not condemn the physical act itself, nor does it limit it to a single social model (classical marriage), but it imperatively requires that it be encapsulated within a framework of responsibility, whether public (Z-W-J) or private (Y-M-N). Slavery and wartime rape are direct violations of this contractual architecture.

Analysis

1. RESTORATION OF LITERARY MEANING (PHYSICAL AND BIOLOGICAL MECHANICS)

“Bring me massive blocks of iron.” Then, when he had symmetrically leveled the interval between the two opposing confinement walls, he issued the instruction: “Activate the blower.” Then, when he had brought the mass to an active thermal state of high intensity, he issued the instruction: “Bring me what to completely empty a molten conductive alloy over.” They then had no grip to adjust themselves and emerge on its surface, and they were no more able to mobilize the effort necessary to drill a passage through it.

The sequence describes a complete heavy metallurgical engineering operation, executed in strictly chained material steps:

  1. Transport and stacking of the structural mass (A-T-Y, Z-B-R, H.-D-D): The operator brings massive blocks of iron, a very high-density material forming an impassable physical barrier, to the work area.
  2. Bilateral leveling in the formwork (S-W-Y in Form III, B-Y-N, S.-D-F): He levels and evens out these metallic masses by establishing a uniform filling in the interval separating two symmetrical opposing walls that deflect any lateral overflow (As-Sadafayn, mechanically functioning like the two sides of a formwork or a foundry mold).
  3. Thermal priming by blower (N-F-KH, J-A.-L, N-Y-R): Once the iron blocks are wedged between these two walls, he issues the instruction to inject pressurized air into the inert mass to initiate and maintain the temperature rise, until the entire iron is brought into a continuous thermal cycle of very high intensity (incandescence).
  4. Sealing by pouring molten alloy (F-R-GH, Q-T.-R): Over this incandescent metallic core, he has a molten conductive material (molten copper or lead) brought, the entire charge of which he empties over the structure. By penetrating the interstices between the white-hot blocks, this alloy ensures a continuous weld and a hermetic thermal joint that transforms the stack of blocks into a monolithic and smooth composite wall.
  5. Neutralization of the two vectors of crossing (T.-W-A., Z.-H-R, N-Q-B): Faced with this completed barrier, external agents fail on the two possible physical planes: they cannot adjust to its smooth wall to emerge on its upper face (Z.-H-R), and they cannot further deploy the prolonged effort required to drill a traversing tunnel through it (N-Q-B). The morphological reduction of the first verb (ٱسْطَٰعُوٓا۟) compared to the complete form of the second (ٱسْتَطَٰعُوا۟) exactly reflects this material gradation: the immediate failure of the surface attempt, followed by the equally total failure of the heavier and longer operation of piercing the mass.

2. AUDIT OF DEVIATIONS AND BREAKDOWN OF CONSENSUS

  1. Geographical reduction of As-Sadafayn (S.-D-F): Traditional exegesis commonly translates As-Sadafayn as "the two mountains" or "the two mountain slopes" facing each other. This gloss crushes the semantic singularity of the text: when the Quran designates a mountain, it uses J-B-L or T.-W-R, never S.-D-F. The root S.-D-F encodes a mechanical function of deflection and framing around a focal point — exactly the role of two opposing walls that contain and channel a flow (like the two valves of a shell or the sides of a foundry mold). Even when natural relief serves as support, reducing the term to a synonym for "mountain" erases the formwork mechanics that make leveling (S-W-Y) and alloy pouring (Q-T.-R) possible.
  2. External geographical and legendary injections: Many classical commentaries (especially in narratives related to ancient chronicles) locate this structure in specific historical sites — such as the Caucasus, Derbent, or certain passes in Central Asia — and graft narrative accounts describing peoples digging the wall every day to see it miraculously reconstitute itself during the night. None of these elements appear in the source text: these are external historical injections that transform a rigorous description of applied metallurgy into a geographical legend.
  3. Lexical flattening of Z.-H-R and the two forms of T.-W-A.: Usual translations uniformly render isṭā'ū and istaṭā'ū as "they could not," and yazharūhu by the simple verb "to climb it." They thus erase two structural precisions: on the one hand, Z.-H-R designates emergence on the visible outer layer (the total absence of asperities preventing any grip on the surface sealed by the alloy); on the other hand, the difference between the abbreviated form (ٱسْطَٰعُوٓا۟) for surface action and the full form (ٱسْتَطَٰعُوا۟) for deep piercing (N-Q-B) marks the difference in mechanical effort between overcoming a wall and piercing a composite block of iron welded with molten metal.
  4. Reduction of N-Y-R to destructive combustion: Translating نَارًۭا solely as "fire" in the common sense obscures the thermodynamic function described here: the iron is not destroyed by flames, it is brought to an active and continuous high-intensity thermal regime to receive the metallic weld (Q-T.-R).

3. LOGICAL AND TOPOLOGICAL CONCLUSION

Verses 18:96-97 set forth a strict material principle: faced with active factors of degradation and disorder (described as destructive on earth in verse 94), the effective response is neither passive waiting nor a suspension of physical laws, but the concrete mastery of matter, geometry, and thermodynamics. The composite structure built by Dhul-Qarnayn combines the structural density of iron, symmetrical balancing between two confinement walls, controlled thermal energy input by blower, and integral sealing by molten alloy. The resulting double invulnerability — impossibility of emerging on the surface and impossibility of drilling through the mass — stems directly from the quality of physical execution.

Topological anchoring (Surah 18 — Al-Kahf): The surah opens with the affirmation of a perfectly straight architecture (قَيِّمًۭا), free from any torsion or internal defect (غَيْرَ ذِى عِوَجٍ / وَلَمْ يَجْعَل لَّهُۥ عِوَجَا, 18:1-2), and concludes with the exclusive value of concrete works that repair and stabilize durably (عَمَلًۭا صَٰلِحًۭا, 18:110), as opposed to incoherent efforts that dissipate to loss (18:103-104). The metallurgical construction of verses 96-97 embodies this exact rectitude on the material plane: a flawlessly leveled structure without fault or breach, methodically designed to contain degradation. At the same time, the following verse (18:98) reminds us that this barrier remains subject to time and will eventually be flattened at its due date — illustrating that human engineering, however rigorous, remains a temporary application of physical laws established by the Central Authority, and never an absolute guarantee that would supersede them. In the contemporary world, this mechanics directly corresponds to the fundamental principles of composite materials metallurgy and industrial containment engineering: resistance by core density, surface continuity, and suppression of structural failure points.
